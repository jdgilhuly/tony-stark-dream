# Read policies first, quote through partners

For a personal assistant like JARVIS, the best starting point is not a carrier API. It is a **consumer-permissioned policy-data aggregator**, and **Canopy Connect** is the strongest choice: about **400 carrier integrations**, **500+ structured fields** (coverages, limits, deductibles, vehicles, drivers, dec pages, ID cards, claims), webhooks, monitoring, public docs and a **free self-serve sandbox** ([Canopy API](https://www.usecanopy.com/api); [Canopy API plans](https://www.usecanopy.com/api/api-plans)). **Axle** and **MeasureOne** are the documented alternatives. Both publish docs and offer a sandbox or dev account, but both are built mainly for lenders verifying auto and home coverage. Quoting and binding is a different category. Real, well-documented carrier APIs exist: **Coterie** (BOP/GL/PL/WC, two calls from quote to bind), **AmTrust** (WC/BOP/GL/package, OAuth developer portal), **Coalition** and **At-Bay** (cyber), **Pie** (WC), and **Branch** (home/auto/umbrella GraphQL with a staging sandbox). All of them assume the production caller is a **licensed, usually appointed, producer** or holds a signed partner agreement. Herald's index alone counts **86 carriers and MGAs exposing 228 commercial API products** ([Herald Insurance API Index](https://www.heraldai.com/insurance-api-index)). Personal lines are far more closed: GEICO, State Farm and Allstate show no public API, and most agency carriers are reachable only through comparative raters. Our recommendation: build read-only policy ingestion on Canopy's sandbox now. For quotes, use a licensed marketplace embed (MediaAlpha, Matic, EverQuote) or an insurance-as-a-service platform (Boost, Ladder). Integrate Coterie, AmTrust or Branch directly only if you obtain an agency license or sign a partner agreement.

## Four kinds of "insurance API", and only one needs no license

The request mixes things that behave very differently in the market, so it helps to sort the options before comparing them. **Policy-data aggregators** (Canopy, Axle, MeasureOne, Covie) let an end user log into their carrier account and return structured policy data. They are the "Plaid for insurance" model, need only the consumer's consent, and fit JARVIS naturally. **Bureau/prefill databases** (LexisNexis, Verisk, TransUnion, Fenris) return household vehicles, drivers and prior-carrier data from a name and address, with no user login. They are enterprise- and carrier-oriented, and LexisNexis requires customers to **contribute data to its Current Carrier database** before they can order reports ([BriteCore on LexisNexis ADP](https://help.britecore.com/hc/en-us/articles/54273711508115-LexisNexis-Auto-Data-Prefill)). **Direct carrier/MGA APIs** (Coterie, AmTrust, Branch, Progressive and others) quote and often bind, but every one reviewed assumes an appointed agent or a contracted partner. **Multi-carrier and embedded platforms** (Bold Penguin, Applied/EZLynx, Vertafore, Matic, MediaAlpha, Boost, Cover Genius) either serve licensed agencies or act as the licensed entity for you.

The licensing line is the constraint that decides everything else. In the US, selling or binding insurance for compensation requires a producer license. The research found **no carrier that opens production binding to anonymous developers**, even among those with fully public docs ([Coterie docs](https://docs.coterieinsurance.com/); [Coalition Broker API](https://www.coalitioninc.com/brokers/api); [At-Bay API Quickstart](https://developers.at-bay.com/docs/getting-started)). An unlicensed developer therefore has three routes: get its own agency license, sign an embedded or referral agreement with a carrier, or use a platform that holds the appointments. Lead-gen and referral publishers generally operate without a license, but state rules on paid referral fees vary, and the notes did not confirm this with a legal source.

## Canopy leads a small field of policy-data aggregators

**Canopy Connect** is the most complete and most transparent aggregator. It claims **96% market coverage for auto, 91% for homeowners and 90% for standard commercial**. It returns policy numbers, premiums, coverages, limits, deductibles, drivers, vehicles, property details, declaration pages, ID cards, a **7-year claims and driving history**, and commercial loss-run summaries ([Canopy API](https://www.usecanopy.com/api); [Canopy API plans](https://www.usecanopy.com/api/api-plans)). The free Sandbox tier includes all API features, the SDK, white-label and unlimited keys. **Production (Pilot)** adds live data, policy editing, property enrichment and account monitoring. **Enterprise** adds an SLA, custom data retention and volume discounts. Pricing for both paid tiers requires contacting sales ([Canopy API plans](https://www.usecanopy.com/api/api-plans)). Its separate non-API agency plans start at **$120/month** ([Canopy agency pricing](https://www.usecanopy.com/agencies/agency-pricing); [Software Finder](https://softwarefinder.com/insurance-software/canopy-connect)). Canopy covers P&C only. The notes found no life or annuity support, and no per-pull production price anywhere.

**Axle** is the closest peer. Its docs list an embedded consent flow (Ignition), Policy Lookup, Monitoring for cancellations and coverage changes, webhooks, Policy Validation rules, **Policy Actions** that act on a policy for the user, and a sandbox ([Axle docs index](https://docs.axle.insure/llms.txt)). When a login fails, Axle falls back to its own verification agents or to Document AI parsing of an uploaded ID card or dec page ([Axle blog](https://www.axle.insure/blog/the-modern-way-to-verify-auto-insurance-a-guide-to-data-apis)). Its scope is **personal auto and commercial fleet** ([Axle API](https://www.axle.insure/product/api)), so it is weaker than Canopy for home or umbrella. **MeasureOne** covers auto and home through a consented login flow, offers monitoring and a document-upload fallback, and has public docs plus a free dev account ([MeasureOne docs](https://docs.measureone.com/overview/api); [MeasureOne coverage details](https://www.measureone.com/solutions/insurance-details-collection)). Its target market is auto lenders. **Covie** offers consent-based policy access and monitoring with usage-based pricing ([Covie pricing](https://www.covie.com/pricing)), but TheGuarantors acquired it in March 2025 ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/covie/__0cJHNS7gN9_ej2ePGOZh4jr0R9tvEMhZoIaSVZLYZKw)), and its sandbox and docs were not verified. **Trellis**, once a consumer-permissioned auto API with public docs, was **acquired by Gen (Norton/LifeLock) on March 10, 2026**. The press release does not mention the Connect API, so treat it as a legacy option ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/gen-expands-insurance-capabilities-of-engine-gens-secure-financial-wellness-marketplace-302710072.html)).

When users cannot or will not link an account, **dec-page extraction** is the fallback. Axle's Document AI and MeasureOne's upload flow handle it inside their products. **Gaya AI** extracts data from dec pages, PDFs, ACORD forms and carrier portals and returns it through APIs and webhooks, but it is designed for agents ([Gaya](https://gaya.ai/)). Among the bureau vendors, **Fenris** is the most reachable: it publishes public docs and offers auto, property (500+ data points per address), small-business and life prefill from a non-contributory dataset ([Fenris docs](https://documentation.fenrisd.com/); [Fenris property](https://fenrisd.com/property-insurance/)). Bureau data answers "what does this household probably have," not "what does my policy actually say." It is useful for prefilling quotes, not for a personal policy vault.

## Coterie, Coalition and At-Bay set the bar for open carrier APIs

Commercial and small-business carriers are much more API-forward than personal-lines carriers. **Coterie** is the benchmark. Its public REST docs, OpenAPI spec and Postman collection cover bindable quotes, bind/issue with **Stripe payment tokens**, NAICS lookup, policy documents, and webhooks for issuance, cancellation and premium changes ([Coterie Docs](https://docs.coterieinsurance.com/); [API Evangelist profile](https://github.com/api-evangelist/coterie)). The minimal embedded flow is **two calls** ([Coterie Quick Start](https://coterieinsurance.com/blog/quick-start-quote-bind-small-business-insurance-with-coterie/)). In 2025 it reported a **92% success rate on bindable BOP and GL quotes** and a partner integration finished in three weeks ([Coterie 2025 recap](https://coterieinsurance.com/newsroom/press-releases/coterie-insurance-caps-2025/)). Access is limited to "appointed agents and digital partners."

**AmTrust** runs a Commercial Lines API for appetite, quote and bind across **WC, BOP, GL and Commercial Package**, with **300+ bind-online-eligible class codes**, a developer portal and OAuth 2.0 with 4-hour tokens. It documents no document, payment or COI endpoints ([API Evangelist AmTrust profile](https://github.com/api-evangelist/amtrust-financial-services)). In practice most traffic reaches it through Appulate, Semsee, Tarmika, IBQ and CoverForce, and the practical access route is an AmTrust agency appointment or an appointed aggregator ([AmTrust/Appulate](https://amtrustfinancial.com/blog/small-business/amtrust-partners-with-appulate-workers-comp-quotes); [CoverForce API Index](https://www.coverforce.com/carriers/api-index)).

Cyber is the most developer-friendly niche. **Coalition** lets partners "rate, quote, bind, and manage renewals entirely through APIs" for cyber, D&O, EPL and Tech E&O. It provides sandbox accounts, a dedicated implementation manager and a **2–4 week average integration**, and more than 200,000 companies have been quoted through it ([Coalition Broker API](https://www.coalitioninc.com/brokers/api)). **At-Bay** publishes open docs with a demo environment at `api-demo.at-bay.com/v2`, JWT auth, and async quote polling with **P90 readiness of 40 seconds**. Production submissions go through broker-of-record clearance ([At-Bay Quickstart](https://developers.at-bay.com/docs/getting-started)). **Cowbell** offers bindable cyber quotes through a developer portal and a co-branded storefront ([Cowbell API quoting](https://cowbell.insure/api-quoting/)). **Pie** covers workers' comp with an appetite checker, indication, quote, document upload and Swagger docs, and issues credentials on request ([Pie Agency API](https://www.pieinsurance.com/agency/api)).

Larger carriers expose portals but keep them gated. **Travelers** offers commercial quoting, policy management and claims APIs at developer.travelers.com ([Travelers API Portal](https://developer.travelers.com/s/)). **Nationwide** exposes Commercial Quote, Wholesale Express and Personal Lines Servicing APIs ([Nationwide product catalog](https://developer.nationwide.com/product-catalog)). **Chubb Studio** is a B2B2C portal with APIs and SDKs for banks, fintech and retail ([Chubb](https://news.chubb.com/2023-11-14-Chubb-Studio-Expands-its-Digital-Integration-Capabilities-with-New-B2B2C-Developer-Portal)). **ERGO NEXT** (formerly NEXT Insurance) has a capable partner API for quote, bind, audits and COIs, used by Gusto and Square, but it has **no public portal, spec or sandbox** ([Supergood report card](https://supergood.ai/api-report-card/next-insurance)). **biBERK** does not appoint retail agents at all and works only through wholesalers ([biBERK partners](https://www.biberk.com/become-a-biberk-partner)).

## Personal lines stay locked behind partnerships and raters

Only a handful of personal-lines carriers publish usable docs. **Branch** is the standout. Its public GraphQL "Quote to Bind" API (`requestQuoteV2`, `addCar`, `addDriver`, `requestBind`, then policy and document retrieval) covers **home, auto, renters and umbrella**, uses API-key auth, and runs a **staging sandbox with test users and payment methods**. Partners can also hand the user off to Branch's own checkout at any step ([Branch API Docs](https://docs.v2.api.ourbranch.com/)). **Steadily** (landlord insurance) has public ReDoc docs and three tiers: widgets, address-only estimates, and a full REST quote/bind/policy-status API for licensed partners ([Steadily Partner API](https://www.steadily.com/api)). **Progressive** offers an "Embedded Direct" auto API that returns an *estimated* rate in all 50 states, with embedded home and renters options. The consumer then completes the purchase with Progressive ([Progressive Embedded Integrations](https://integrations.progressive.com/)). **Neptune Flood** exposes a Quick Quote v4 API that deep-links into its agent portal for bind. It handles **20,000+ partner quotes a day** and launched a ChatGPT quoting app in March 2026 ([Neptune S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2067129/000162828026033573/neptuneflood-sx1.htm); [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/03/13/neptune-flood-launches-insurance-quoting-app-in-chatgpt/)). **Lemonade** still lists an API doc portal for renters and homeowners, but the material that could be verified dates from 2017 ([Lemonade API](https://www.lemonade.com/api); [Lemonade blog](https://www.lemonade.com/blog/introducing-lemonade-insurance-api/)).

Life and specialty lines follow the same pattern. **Ladder** has public docs for an embeddable JS term-life flow (up to $3M, no exam), plus Quoter and prefill APIs ([Ladder API](https://ladderlife.github.io/api/)). **Ethos** offers partner APIs from quote to signed policy, but by request only ([Ethos API](https://www.ethos.com/api/)). **Bestow** launched a Protect API for embedded term life but has since pivoted toward SaaS for life carriers ([Bestow API partners](https://www.bestow.com/api-partners/); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/bestow-vs-ethos-technologies)). **Haven Life stopped issuing policies in March 2024** ([Policygenius](https://www.policygenius.com/life-insurance/reviews/haven-life/)). **Trupanion** (pet) and **Assurant** (renters) run Azure-hosted partner portals behind approval ([API Evangelist Trupanion](https://github.com/api-evangelist/trupanion); [Assurant housing portal](https://housing-apis.developer.assurant.com/)). **Root**, **Clearcover**, **Hippo** and **Obie** embed insurance only through bespoke deals. Root's Carvana checkout, for example, passed 200,000 policies in April 2026 ([Carvana IR](https://investors.carvana.com/news-releases/2026/04-14-2026-140928073)).

The notes found no public developer API for **GEICO, State Farm, Allstate, Safeco, Travelers personal lines, Mercury, National General, Foremost, Dairyland, Bristol West or Stillwater**. For these carriers, access runs through agency comparative raters and ACORD/IVANS connectivity ([Fern](https://buildwithfern.com/post/insurance-carriers-build-api-partner-portals)). These raters are all gated to licensed agencies. **Vertafore PL Rating** connects to **300+ carriers in 48 states** and requires a Developer Portal contract ([Vertafore PL Rating](https://www.vertafore.com/products/insurance-comparative-rater/pl-rating); [API Evangelist Vertafore](https://github.com/api-evangelist/vertafore)). **Applied** owns EZLynx, Tarmika, Ivans and Ask Kodiak, so a single Applied partnership covers much of independent-agency rating ([Applied acquires Tarmika](https://www1.appliedsystems.com/en-us/news/press-releases/2022/applied-systems-acquires-tarmika/)).

## Where JARVIS should start

**Step 1: policy vault.** Sign up for Canopy's free sandbox and build a "connect your insurer" flow that pulls the user's auto, home and umbrella policies into JARVIS. That gives the assistant coverage limits, deductibles, renewal dates, premiums, ID cards and claims history to answer questions like "am I covered for a rental car?" or "when does my home policy renew?". Canopy's webhooks and monitoring can drive renewal and cancellation alerts through JARVIS's existing notification service. Add document upload with a dec-page parser as a fallback for carriers the aggregator cannot reach. Use Axle or MeasureOne as a second source if coverage gaps appear, remembering that both are tuned for auto. This step needs **no insurance license**, because the user consents to their own data.

**Step 2: quotes without a license.** Use referral embeds instead of carrier APIs. MediaAlpha's Embedded Quote Experience, EverQuote or SmartFinancial affiliate programs, or a Matic partnership let JARVIS show real multi-carrier rates while a licensed agency handles solicitation and binding ([MediaAlpha](https://mediaalpha.com/embedded-quote-experience/); [Matic](https://matic.com/partners/)). Progressive's embedded auto estimate and Branch's hand-off URLs work the same way for single carriers. For narrower products, Boost (renters, pet) and Ladder (term life) act as the licensed entity and publish docs a developer can start from today.

**Step 3: direct quote and bind, only if you license up.** If JARVIS becomes a real distribution business, obtain a producer/agency license and appointments. Then Coterie (small business), AmTrust (WC/BOP), Coalition or At-Bay (cyber) and Branch (personal bundles) are the cleanest direct integrations, and Bold Penguin's DeX ai MCP server is the most AI-agent-native path into commercial lines. Budget legal review for state referral-fee rules before charging for any insurance-related placement.

## Conclusion

The "insurance API" market is two markets that rarely overlap. Reading a policy is a consent problem: aggregators solved it, it works across nearly every carrier, and a solo developer can reach it today. Writing a policy is a licensing problem, and no amount of good documentation removes it. Coterie's and Branch's open specs make the engineering easy, but production access still runs through appointments. JARVIS should therefore treat itself as a policyholder's agent before it tries to act as an insurance agent.

The more interesting signal is where the market is heading. Axle now markets "AI agents" that take policy actions, Bold Penguin ships an MCP server for bind, and Neptune quotes inside ChatGPT. Carriers and exchanges are starting to expose insurance as tools for AI agents, which suggests the licensed-intermediary layer will increasingly offer agent-callable endpoints. An assistant built on a clean policy-data model now will be ready to plug into those endpoints without re-architecting. The unknowns worth watching are production pricing for aggregators (no provider publishes it), Lemonade's API status, and whether Trellis Connect survives inside Gen.
